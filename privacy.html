Mutram privacy policy

Last updated 27 October 2026. Applies to the Mutram Android app and the Mutram web app.

Mutram helps you keep track of your money, health, home, travel, reading and day. It is built so that your information stays with you: Mutram has no server. Nothing you keep in Mutram is sent to the people who made it, and they cannot see it.

What Mutram reads, and where

  • Text messages (SMS), on Android, if you allow it: bank, payment, ticket and pharmacy messages, read on your phone to record payments, bills, journeys and medicines. Personal messages and one-time passwords are ignored. Message text is never uploaded.
  • Notifications, if you turn on notification access: payment, bank and shopping app notifications only, read on your phone.
  • Gmail, read-only, if you connect it: order, bank alert, ticket, hotel and pharmacy emails, read directly by your phone from Google. Mutram never sends, changes or deletes email.
  • Calendar, read-only, if you allow it: your events, shown in your day.
  • Physical activity, if you allow it: the phone's own step count.
  • Camera and photos, when you choose to: prescriptions, bills, tickets and book covers, read on your phone.
  • Location, only if you turn it on: to answer a family member's "Where are you?" and, if you choose, to note which town you are in a few times a day. Only the town is kept.

Where your information is kept

In the app's private storage on your phone (or your browser, for the web app). If you sign in with Google, a backup of your setup (without message text) is kept in a hidden app folder in your own Google Drive, which only Mutram on your devices can open. You can lock it with a passphrase.

What can leave your phone, and only when you turn it on

  • Family sharing. If you share Veedu (lists, chores, household help, documents), trips (without prices) or the family's medicines (without reports), they go to a file in your Google Drive, shared only with the email addresses you enter. You can stop sharing at any time.
  • Weather. The name of a city you are travelling to is sent to Open-Meteo to get its forecast.
  • AI assistant. If you add your own API key for an AI provider, a summary of the relevant figures (no message text, account numbers or people's names) is sent to that provider when you ask a question. Questions about trips, purchases and spending are answered on the phone.
  • Reports you send. Problem reports and samples of messages Mutram could not read are masked (digits and names hidden) and go only where you choose to share them.
  • Updates. The Android app reads a small file from Mutram's public releases page on GitHub to see if there is a new version. Nothing about you is sent.

Google user data

Mutram uses Google sign-in, Google Drive and (if you connect it) Gmail read-only access only to provide the features above: your backup, family sharing you set up, and reading booking, order, bank and pharmacy emails on your device. Google user data is not transferred to anyone else, not used for advertising, and not read by people. Mutram's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your choices

  • Every permission is optional and can be turned off in your phone's settings.
  • You can sign out of Google, disconnect Gmail and stop any sharing from Mutram's settings.
  • Uninstalling Mutram removes everything it kept on your phone. Your Drive backup can be deleted from Google Drive (Settings, Manage apps).

Children

Mutram is meant for adults managing their own and their family's affairs.
